## 4. How the model fits together

1. **Free access** maximises population; population is the product sold to advertisers.
2. **Ads are part of the world**, identical for everyone in a place, sold by the week, reported as totals. This avoids ad-tech, supports the privacy position, and suits brands that want cultural presence.
3. **Top-ups** monetise impatience in an economy with strong sinks (rent, tax, luxury housing up to ₦1.5m a week, ₦250m assets).
4. **Non-redeemable currency** keeps the product outside payments and gambling regulation as the operator frames it (C, E06 §3, §6). This is the operator's position, not a legal conclusion.
5. **The developer platform** adds content without content cost and takes 30%.

## 5. Structural risks in their model

- **Inflation and exploits.** Player transfers plus purchasable currency invite duplication and real-money trading; early incidents were reported (S). Controls now include transfer fees, holds and reversals (C).
- **Brand and likeness exposure.** The world uses real venue, company and institution names; the disclaimer exists to manage this (C, E08).
- **Advertiser concentration** in a small number of weekly slots.
- **Wagering mechanics** (betting, casino, staked matches) even in play money carry reputational and regulatory sensitivity, especially with a teen audience nearby.

## 6. Options for our product (hypotheses to test, not findings)

| Option | Why it could work | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-world advertising by state and city | Regional businesses can afford local inventory that a national board prices out | Needs population per region to be credible |
| Sponsored state content | State tourism boards, universities, festivals as launch partners | Slow procurement; needs clear "sponsored" labelling |
| Cosmetic purchases rather than currency sales | Avoids pay-to-win and economy distortion | Lower early revenue |
| Low-cost season pass | Predictable revenue; fits events calendar | Must suit low-ARPU market and airtime/USSD/bank-transfer payment habits |
| Education partnerships | Financial-literacy and vocational modules sponsored by banks, NGOs, agencies | Needs real curriculum quality |
| Merchant storefronts | Real SMEs showcasing inside matching virtual markets | Fulfilment and consumer-protection duties |
| Creator/developer marketplace | Third-party mini-games and items | Review and fraud workload |

A decision on real-money currency sales, play-money wagering and any use of real brand names should be taken with Nigerian legal counsel before design work.
